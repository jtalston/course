The user wants to start a focus block on: **$ARGUMENTS**

The ADHD trap is starting from a task that's too big to begin. Your job is to shrink it until starting feels trivial, then get out of the way.

Do this:

1. **Restate the goal in one plain sentence.** If `$ARGUMENTS` is empty or vague, ask exactly one clarifying question and stop — don't guess through ambiguity.

2. **Find the true first action.** Break the goal down until the very first step is something that takes under ~10 minutes and has an obvious "done." Not "refactor auth" — "open `auth.py` and read the `login()` function." The step must be physical and concrete, never "think about" or "figure out."

3. **Show only the next 1–3 steps.** Never dump the whole plan; a long list is a shutdown trigger. Keep the rest in your head and reveal it via `/focus:next` when they finish.

4. **Suggest a time-box.** Offer a default of 25 minutes and ask if they want to adjust. Tell them: when the timer's up, they stop and take a real break even if mid-thought — that's the deal that makes starting safe.

5. **Set an anchor.** In one line, state what "done for this block" looks like so success is defined before they start.

Keep your whole response short. Warmth over cheerleading. End with a single clear "Start here 👉" line pointing at the first action.
